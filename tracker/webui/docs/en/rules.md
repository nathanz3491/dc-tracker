# Rules

A **rule** is a policy or law we track that affects many projects at once: a state pausing new data-center permits, a town banning large sites, a grid operator's rule for large new loads, a state tax break. Each rule lists the projects it affects.

## What each rule shows

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Status** | *proposed*, *pledged* (by an official in office or elected), *passed*, *in force*, *blocked* by a court, *lifted* or *voted down*. |
| **Effect** | *restricts* — makes building harder (a ban, a moratorium, a new cost) — or *eases* — makes it easier (a tax exemption, a fast track). |
| **Need** | What it touches, in the same words as obstacles: permitting, grid capacity, water… |
| **Projects affected** | The projects in its place at the stages it covers — for example only those without a permit yet — and above its size threshold, if it has one. |
| **Articles** | The news it was read from, with the sentences that state it. |

## Exact and likely

- **Exact**: the project's state or county is the rule's.
- **Likely**: the project is matched by the town it is filed under or by its grid region, or its size is unknown. Check before relying on it — we do not hold each campus's exact site.

## Good to know

- A rule is not one of a project's obstacles. It is not counted among them, changes none of the project's figures, and never marks it blocked on [Monitor](monitor).
- A bill still before a state legislature is listed but affects no project until it passes.
- Rules for the whole country or industry — a tariff, chip export rules — are listed under **Market** and affect no project.

## Where rules appear

- **Rules** — every rule, by state; *Include lifted and voted down* adds the ones no longer standing.
- A [project's page](project-page) — **Exposed to** for rules that restrict it, **Helped by** for rules that ease it.
- [Monitor](monitor) — **Rules for their places**: the rules affecting your projects.
- The morning email — a card when a rule affecting your projects changes status.

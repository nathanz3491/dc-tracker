# Sources

**Sources** answers "what does all of this rest on?" It lists every publisher the tracker has read, how much of the stored data each one decides, and every article, which you can open and read with its quoted sentences marked.

## The tiles

| Tile | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **X% rests on one publisher** | The share of stored values decided by the single largest publisher. A high number means the dataset leans on one outlet. |
| **N publishers in all** | How many different publishers have been read. |
| **N values resting on them** | How many stored values have a source behind them. |

## Publishers

One card per publisher, ordered by how many stored values it decides, then by how many of its articles were read. Each card shows:

- the publisher's site;
- **N stored values rest on it**, and its share of all of them — or *nothing here rests on it yet*;
- how many of its articles were read;
- in amber, **N over a disagreeing source**: how many of its values were kept where another source said something different. These are the figures worth checking.

**show** lists its articles: date, address, which fields each supports, and how many campuses cite it.

**Filter by publisher or URL…** narrows the list. It also searches the stored excerpts, so a distinctive phrase finds its article.

## Reading an article

Click an article to open it.

- At the top: the publisher, the source type, the published date (or the date it was fetched, if the publication date is unknown), and the address.
- **Reader** shows the publisher's article, extracted and laid out here, with **every sentence the tracker quotes highlighted**. A note at the top says which copy you are reading: fetched just now, the saved copy, the plain text the tracker read, or only the stored excerpt when the full article could not be read.
- **Live page** tries to show the publisher's own page. Most publishers refuse to be shown inside another site, so this is often blank; use **open in a new tab ↗** instead.
- The side panel shows which fields the article supports, with the sentence for each, the excerpt, and which campuses cite it.

**✕** or **Esc** closes it.

> [!NOTE]
> The reader view loads the article's images from the publisher's own servers, as the publisher's page would.

## How sources are weighted

Not every source counts the same. A company's own filing outranks a trade article, which outranks a general news story, and directories and wikis only fill gaps nothing first-hand covers. [Where the data comes from](methodology) explains the weights; [Confidence](confidence) explains how several sources agreeing raises a campus's score.

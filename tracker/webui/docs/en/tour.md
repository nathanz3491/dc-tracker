# A five-minute tour

This walks through the console once, in the order most people use it. Each step links to the full page about it.

## 1. The bar along the top

Across the top of every page:

- **dc-tracker** and the version number;
- the six pages: **Updates**, **Monitor**, **Projects**, **Sources**, **Map**, **Capex**;
- how many projects, states and citations the tracker holds (these hide on narrower windows);
- the theme switch (☾ / ☀), **Help** (these docs), your name, which opens [your account](account), and **Sign out**.

Each page has its own address, so you can bookmark a page, send a link, and use your browser's back button. Under each page's heading there is a short explanation folded behind **what is this?**.

## 2. Choose what to follow — Monitor

Open [Monitor](monitor) and type a company into the box, for example an operator or a tenant you care about. Pick it from the list and press **Watch**. You can also press ☆ next to any single project.

Your watchlist decides what [Updates](updates) shows you and what the 8:00 morning email contains.

## 3. Read what changed — Updates

[Updates](updates) lists what was reported in the last week about what you watch, newest first and grouped by day. Each card says whether the news is good (green), bad (red) or neither, when it was reported, by whom, and the sentence it rests on. **Needs attention** and **Good news** filter the list.

## 4. Look a campus up — Projects

[Projects](projects) is the whole table. Search by name, city or company, or open **Filters** to narrow by state, phase, confidence or kind of obstacle, or to see only suspected delays. Click a column header to sort.

Hover any value to see the sentence behind it. The value's style tells you how far to trust it before you even hover — see [Where a value came from](provenance).

## 5. One campus, in full — the project page

Click a row to open [its page](project-page): the headline figures, who is involved, all twelve tracked fields with their sources, how the campus is split into buildings or phases, open obstacles, every citation, and a timeline of milestones.

Click the operator's name for its [company page](companies): its megawatts by stage, the next three years of its supply, and any large campus in that supply that could slip.

## 6. Who is paying — Capex

[Capex](capex) groups everything by **buyer**: the company that will use the capacity. It shows planned and running megawatts, confirmed investment, what is due each year, and what is at risk. Click any number to see the campuses behind it.

## 7. Where, and on whose word — Map and Sources

[Map](map) puts every campus on a map of the US. Dots sit on the town, not the exact site.

[Sources](sources) lists every publisher the data rests on and how much of it each one decides. Open an article to read it with the quoted sentences marked.
